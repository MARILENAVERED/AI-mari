# Research Question

**Status:** Draft — created to test `literature-scout` (Step 3 test run).
**Last updated:** 2026-09-08

## Question

What are the most recent peer-reviewed studies (2022–2026) evaluating EGFR
mutation subtypes and their association with treatment response, acquired
resistance, and clinical outcomes in patients with non-small cell lung
cancer (NSCLC)?

## Scope framework

- **Population:** Adult patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
  harboring EGFR mutations (common: exon 19 deletion, L858R; uncommon:
  exon 20 insertion, G719X, L861Q, S768I, compound mutations).
- **Intervention/exposure:** EGFR-mutation subtype as a variable, generally
  in the context of EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) therapy
  (osimertinib and other generations).
- **Comparator:** Other EGFR mutation subtypes, or wild-type EGFR, where
  reported.
- **Outcomes:** Treatment response (ORR, PFS, OS), acquired resistance
  mechanisms, and clinical outcomes associated with specific mutation
  subtypes.
- **Study types:** Clinical studies preferred (cohort studies, clinical
  trials, retrospective/prospective analyses); mechanistic/basic studies on
  resistance mechanisms may be included if directly tied to clinical
  outcome data.
- **Date range:** 2022–2026.
- **Language:** English.

## Inclusion criteria

- Peer-reviewed, published (not preprint-only) studies.
- Human NSCLC patients with molecularly confirmed EGFR mutation status.
- Reports treatment response, resistance, or clinical outcome data
  stratified by EGFR mutation subtype.

## Exclusion criteria

- Non-NSCLC cancers, animal-only or purely in vitro studies with no clinical
  correlate.
- Case reports (single patient) unless no other evidence exists on a rare
  subtype.
- Studies not reporting subtype-level (only pooled "EGFR-mutant") data.
- Preprints, conference abstracts without full text, or non-peer-reviewed
  sources.
